# Model card: lead conversion scoring

## Purpose
Rank the leads a seller could call this month so the most likely converters are called first, and
explain each lead's rank in plain language. Scores are refreshed monthly.

## Data
`gold_lead_features`: 41,176 outbound calls (May 2008 to November 2010) with pre-call features only.
`call_duration_sec` is excluded because it is known only after the call and leaks the outcome.

## How it was validated
A **rolling monthly backtest**: each of 19 months (March 2009 to November 2010) is scored by a model
trained only on earlier months. Random train/test splits were rejected because they would let the
model learn from future market conditions; on this data a random split reports AUC 0.80, while the
honest out-of-time result is about 0.61.

## Decisions and the experiments behind them

| Question | Result | Decision |
|---|---|---|
| Retrain monthly or train once? | Monthly retraining AUC 0.613 vs 0.548 for a model trained once on 2008 to early 2009 data, which drifts toward chance in 2010 | Retrain every month |
| Weight recent months more? | In LightGBM experiments, the expanding window scored AUC 0.611, half-life weighting (3 or 6 months) 0.581 to 0.588, and a 6-month sliding window 0.600 with much worse calibration | Use all past months, equally weighted |
| Which model? | Logistic regression AUC 0.613, LightGBM 0.611 | Logistic regression: equal accuracy, simpler, and its explanations are exact |
| Better than the seller's rule of thumb? | Rule ("past successes first") AUC 0.568 | Yes, the champion beat the rule in all 19 backtest months |
| Recalibrate on recent months? | Platt scaling on the last 3 or 6 training months raised the Brier score from 0.167 to 0.199 or worse, because recent months were not representative of the next one | Keep raw probabilities and document the limitation |

## Performance (out of time)
See `docs/results.md` for the full tables, which are regenerated by `scripts/run_model.py`.

- **Ranking:** AUC 0.613 within month. Calling the top 20% of leads finds about 29% of conversions
  across the full backtest, and in the latest three months the top 20% found 32% and the top 30% found 48%.
- **What drives conversion overall:** most of the predictive power in this data is about *when* to
  call (interest rates and employment conditions shift conversion from about 5% to over 50%), not
  *whom* to call within a month. Lead-level ranking adds a real but modest lift.

## Limitations
- **Calibration at the extremes.** Out of time, the top score decile averages 84% predicted vs 58%
  actual, and the bottom decile 5% predicted vs 15% actual. Use scores for ranking. Treat
  top-decile probabilities as optimistic when forecasting conversions.
- **Regime change.** The 2008 to 2010 period includes a financial crisis and a complete change in
  campaign strategy. Results may not hold under different conditions without retraining.
- **Fairness.** Age and job are used as features. Before production use, check that call volume and
  outcomes are not unfairly skewed across age groups.

## Explanations
Each lead gets up to three reasons. For a linear model, coefficient times the lead's deviation from
the mean is the exact SHAP value. The mean is taken over the **same month's leads**, so economic
indicators that are identical for everyone that month cancel out, and the reasons answer the
seller's question: why is this lead above the others I could call?
